The_Hero was born under the mark of prophecy, and it haunted them all their life. Before they could understand the words spoken over their cradle, strangers had already decided what they would become. Priests watched for signs, rulers feared what the prophecy might mean, and their family tried in vain to give them an ordinary childhood. As The_Hero grew older, every triumph was treated as proof of destiny and every mistake as evidence that disaster was approaching. By the time they came of age, The_Hero wanted only one thing: to discover whether they were truly bound by fate, or whether a person could choose a life beyond the story written for them.

The_Hero grew up in a quiet village at the edge of a vast and dangerous wilderness. Their days were spent tending animals, helping neighbors, and listening to travelers tell impossible stories about ruined kingdoms and forgotten roads. Everyone in the village believed The_Hero would live and die within sight of the same hills where they had been born. Then, one autumn evening, a wounded stranger arrived carrying a broken sword and a message meant for someone who had been dead for twenty years. The message bore The_Hero's family name, and by sunrise their ordinary life had ended.

The_Hero was the last surviving child of a disgraced noble house. Their family name had once opened palace doors, but after accusations of treason it became something spoken only in whispers. Raised in hiding by loyal retainers, The_Hero learned to conceal their identity and never ask too many questions about the night their parents died. On their eighteenth birthday, an old servant finally revealed a sealed chest containing letters, a signet ring, and evidence that the family's downfall had been engineered. The_Hero now faced a choice between the safety of anonymity and the dangerous task of reclaiming a stolen legacy.

The_Hero had always been told never to enter the forest beyond the old stone boundary. The villagers claimed monsters lived there, though no one could agree on what the monsters looked like or when they had last been seen. As a child, The_Hero obeyed out of fear; as an adult, they obeyed mostly out of habit. That changed when their younger sibling vanished beneath the trees during a sudden storm. With night approaching and the villagers refusing to cross the boundary, The_Hero took a lantern, an old hunting knife, and the forbidden path into the forest where every childhood warning waited to become real.

The_Hero began life as an orphan in the crowded streets of a great city. They survived by running errands, picking pockets, and learning which alleys belonged to which gangs. Their greatest talent was not fighting but noticing things: unlocked windows, nervous merchants, hidden symbols, and lies people thought no one could see. One night, while stealing food from a wealthy stranger, The_Hero discovered a map tattooed onto the inside of the stranger's arm. By morning the stranger was dead, the city guard was searching for a murderer, and The_Hero realized that the same map had somehow appeared on their own skin.

The_Hero was trained from childhood to become a warrior for a cause they never chose. Their teachers praised obedience, endurance, and sacrifice, insisting that personal desires were weaknesses that endangered everyone around them. For years The_Hero believed them, becoming one of the finest fighters of their generation. Then came a battle in which they were ordered to destroy a village suspected of sheltering enemies. When The_Hero discovered that the accusation was false, they refused the command. By sunset they had saved the village, betrayed their commanders, and become a fugitive from the very army that had shaped their entire identity.

The_Hero inherited a mysterious key from a grandparent they barely remembered. It was too large for any ordinary lock and made from a metal that never rusted, warmed, or cooled. For years it sat forgotten in a drawer until The_Hero encountered a door beneath the foundations of an abandoned house. The door had no hinges, no handle, and only one feature: a keyhole shaped exactly like the object they had inherited. When The_Hero turned the key, the door opened not into another room but onto a road beneath a sky filled with unfamiliar stars.

The_Hero had spent years trying to live down the terrible deeds of their famous parent. Wherever they went, people recognized the family resemblance and assumed cruelty, ambition, or treachery must run in the blood. The_Hero deliberately chose a modest life, helping others whenever possible and refusing every opportunity for power. Then word arrived that their parent, long believed dead, had returned and was gathering followers. Worse, the returning tyrant had sent a personal message asking The_Hero to come home and take their rightful place at their side.

The_Hero was an apprentice to a brilliant but impossible master. For years they swept floors, copied manuscripts, prepared ingredients, and waited for the day when their real training would begin. The master always promised that the important lessons would come later, once The_Hero had learned patience. Then one morning the master disappeared, leaving behind a wrecked workshop, a cryptic note, and a magical disaster slowly spreading through the surrounding town. With no one else willing to claim responsibility, The_Hero was forced to use half-understood lessons to solve a crisis their teacher had apparently expected them to face alone.

The_Hero died before their story truly began. At least, that was what everyone believed. They remembered the blade, the cold ground, and the voices fading into darkness, but they also remembered waking days later in a ruined shrine with no wounds and no explanation. Returning home should have been a miracle, yet people recoiled from them, animals refused to approach, and old friends insisted they had watched The_Hero's body burn on a funeral pyre. Somewhere between death and waking, something had changed, and The_Hero set out to discover what had brought them back and what price might eventually be demanded for their second life.
